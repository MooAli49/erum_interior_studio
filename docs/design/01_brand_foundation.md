## 7. Visual Direction

- **Canvas Simplicity:** Clean, open spaces allow real project photography to command attention.
- **Controlled Palette:** Navy provides structure, depth, and gravitas; gold is used with surgical restraint for focal highlights, never as a generic background wash.
- **Editorial Typography:** High-contrast, beautifully proportioned typefaces that treat projects like curated monograph features.

## 10. Typography Direction

Typography is the foundational voice of an architectural brand. It must convey engineering precision and contemporary luxury while performing harmoniously across both **Arabic (RTL)** and **Latin (LTR)** scripts.

### Typographic Principles
1. **Geometric Harmony:** Both scripts should share similar visual weights, vertical proportions (x-height to cap-height), and stroke contrast.
2. **Architectural Clarity:** Avoid calligraphic, overly ornamental, or novelty fonts that conflict with modern spatial design.
3. **Dual-Script Legibility:** The chosen font families must maintain flawless rendering on low-density mobile screens as well as high-resolution desktop monitors.

### Candidate Font Pairings `[PROPOSED CANDIDATES - PENDING SELECTION]`

```
Option A: Modern Architectural (Clean, Contemporary, Engineering Focus)
├── Latin: Plus Jakarta Sans / Inter
└── Arabic: IBM Plex Sans Arabic / Alexandria

Option B: Refined Editorial (Sophisticated, Editorial, Studio Focus)
├── Latin: Outfit / General Sans
└── Arabic: Readex Pro / Noto Sans Arabic
```

| Option | Latin Candidate | Arabic Candidate | Visual Characteristic | Recommendation Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option A (Recommended)** | **Plus Jakarta Sans** | **Alexandria** or **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** | Geometric, crisp apertures, modern architectural feel, excellent bilingual balance. | Ideal for balancing engineering credibility with modern design flair. |
| **Option B (Editorial Modern)** | **Outfit** | **Readex Pro** | Highly geometric, warm, open counters, approachable luxury. | Excellent for content-heavy hubs, videos, and social-first audiences. |
| **Option C (Minimalist Neutral)** | **Inter** | **Noto Sans Arabic** | Maximum neutral legibility, robust system support, zero visual distraction. | Strong fallback if licensing or cross-platform uniformity is paramount. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> No official font has been confirmed yet by the client. The above pairings are technical and aesthetic recommendations based on current brand assets. Final font selection will occur after client review.

## 11. Photography and Image Art Direction

The website's primary visual impact rests on real project photography. Stock photography destroys credibility in the design and execution market.

### Photography Hierarchy `[CONFIRMED PRINCIPLE]`
1. **Completed Real Projects (Priority 1):** Real spaces completed by ERUM. Captured with natural daylight, authentic material textures, and balanced vertical lines (architectural tilt-shift perspective).
2. **Render vs. Reality Showcases (Priority 2):** Side-by-side or interactive comparison showing the initial 3D design next to the actual built space. This is ERUM’s strongest conversion tool.
3. **Execution & Craftsmanship on Site (Priority 3):** High-resolution captures of gypsum installation, electrical conduits, plumbing pressure tests, marble laying, and custom woodwork in progress. Demonstrates engineering rigor.
4. **Engineer & Team in Action (Priority 4):** Authentic, candid photography of Eng. Abdelrahman reviewing architectural drawings, conducting site inspections, and directing craftspeople.

### Image Art Direction Rules `[PROPOSED]`
- **Lighting:** Favor warm, natural architectural illumination. Avoid harsh artificial flash photography.
- **Composition:** Maintain level horizons and vertical lines (two-point perspective). Frame spaces with foreground context (e.g., viewing a living space past a custom timber screen).
- **Post-Processing:** Natural color grading that faithfully reflects real materials (wood tones, stone veining, fabric textures). Avoid aggressive HDR or oversaturated filters.
- **Image Metadata Tagging:** Every project photo should clearly indicate whether it is:
  - `[REAL PHOTOGRAPHY]`
  - `[3D RENDER / CONCEPT]`
  - `[WORK IN PROGRESS / SITE]`

## 12. Layout and Composition Principles

### 1. Architectural Grid & Proportions
Layouts should be constructed on an intentional 8px/12-column modular grid. Proportions should echo architectural elevations: generous vertical spacing, clean aligned columns, and deliberate asymmetrical balance.

### 2. Generous Whitespace as a Design Element
Whitespace is not "empty space"; it is the luxury frame that separates premium studios from chaotic contractor advertisements. By allowing 80px–120px padding between major desktop sections, each project feels distinct and valuable.

### 3. Asymmetric Editorial Rhythm
To prevent visual fatigue, the layout alternates between:
- Full-bleed panoramic hero imagery.
- Structured two-column content blocks (text + image).
- Three-column project and service cards.
- Data-rich technical grids (BOQ breakdown, project specifications).

## 13. Motion and Interaction Principles

### Restrained Micro-Interactions `[PROPOSED]`
Motion should feel smooth, physical, and purposeful, never frivolous or distracting.

- **Image Reveals:** Smooth 300ms–400ms subtle opacity and transform shifts (`translateY(8px)` to `translateY(0)`) as cards enter the viewport.
- **Hover Transitions:** Gentle 200ms easing on interactive cards (slight image scale of 1.02x within an `overflow: hidden` frame, accompanied by an elevation shadow shift).
- **Tab & Filter Switching:** Smooth cross-fades rather than abrupt content jumps.
- **Render-to-Reality Sliders:** Interactive touch/drag handles allowing clients to slide between 3D design and real photography with fluid 60fps response.
- **Reduced Motion:** Strict support for `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` disabling all non-essential transform and transition animations for vestibular comfort.

## 14. Arabic and English Brand Presentation

### Bidirectional Parity `[CONFIRMED REQUIREMENT]`
The website must treat **Arabic (RTL)** and **English (LTR)** as co-equal first-class experiences. Arabic is not an afterthought, and English is not an unstyled clone.

### Key Implementation Principles
1. **RTL Mirroring of Structure:**
   - Visual flow mirrors naturally: navigation items, brand mark, back/forward arrows, and timeline steps reverse direction.
   - Text alignment defaults to `right` in Arabic and `left` in English.
2. **Directional Non-Mirroring Exceptions:**
   - **Phone & WhatsApp Numbers:** Always presented in standard international LTR format (e.g., `+20 1X XXXX XXXX`).
   - **Media Controls:** Video play, pause, progress bars, and volume sliders remain standardized LTR.
   - **Numerical Dimensions & Units:** Units are formatted naturally in each language (e.g., `350 m²` in English, `٣٥٠ م²` or `350 م²` in Arabic depending on client numeral preference).
3. **Typography Adjustment for Arabic:**
   - Arabic type requires slightly increased line-height (`line-height: 1.6` to `1.75`) compared to Latin (`1.4` to `1.5`) due to character ascenders, descenders, and diacritics.
   - Body font size in Arabic should typically be set 1px–2px larger than the Latin equivalent to achieve equal optical weight.

## 15. Visual Do's and Don'ts

| Category | DO | DON'T |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Color Usage** | Use Navy (`#203D73`) as the dominant anchor; use Gold (`#F5A000`) for delicate micro-accents, badges, and focus triggers. | Do NOT flood entire page backgrounds with solid gold or use heavy yellow-gold gradients. |
| **Contrast & a11y** | Ensure all text passes WCAG AA (4.5:1). Darken gold when used as text on white backgrounds. | Do NOT place light gold text on white backgrounds or dark navy text on dark backgrounds. |
| **Imagery** | Prioritize genuine ERUM project photography, site progress captures, and honest render-vs-reality comparisons. | Do NOT use generic Western stock photos of luxury mansions that ERUM did not design or build. |
| **Typography** | Use disciplined, geometric sans-serif typefaces with balanced Arabic/Latin weights. | Do NOT mix more than two font families; avoid traditional ornate calligraphy for UI body text. |
| **Layout** | Embrace generous whitespace, structured margins, and architectural grid alignment. | Do NOT cram multiple banners, competing badges, and floating promotional stickers onto the screen. |
| **Motion** | Keep micro-interactions subtle, fast (150ms–300ms), and physics-based. | Do NOT use continuous looping animations, bouncing buttons, or slow entrance transitions. |
